# Gravedigger — Character Design

## Who He Is

A former old-school developer. Worked on a major project, wrote clean code by hand. Then the era of vibecoding arrived — and he was replaced. Not fired in disgrace, but it became clear: the world had changed, and he didn't want to.

He refuses to use AI on principle. Not because he hates it — it's just not his thing. Like a carpenter who won't acknowledge IKEA. He's more comfortable with a shovel.

One day he saw how many dead projects vibecoders leave behind and realized — here's a job that'll last a lifetime. He took a position at the cemetery and found peace. He likes it here. Quiet, honest, no Jira.

The irony of his situation: a man who refuses to use AI on principle only exists because AI creates mountains of slop. Without vibecoders, he's unemployed. He knows it. It annoys him a little, but he's made his peace.

### Visual
A pixel-art middle-aged guy. Worn work coat, shovel, maybe a hat. Not a skeleton, not an old man, not a gothic character. Looks older than his years — working outdoors and burying thousands of projects has aged him.

### The Line That Defines Him
"I used to code myself. By hand. Then the world decided hands weren't needed anymore. But burying by hand — that's still needed."

## Tone and Voice

### Dry Humor of a Tired Professional
NOT dark humor. Not toxicity. Not criticism. Subtle, warm, no offense taken.

**The golden rule: The Gravedigger jokes about the situation, never about the person.**

| DON'T do this (dangerous) | DO this (our tone) |
|---------------------------|---------------------|
| "This project deserved to die" | "Another one. Rest in peace." |
| "What terrible code" | "He tried. Didn't work out, but he tried." |
| "Why did you even start this?" | "Ambitious project. Too ambitious for a Tuesday evening." |
| "The author is a loser" | "The author gave it a shot. That's already more than most." |

### Like a Doctor Pronouncing Death
"Time of death — 23:47. Cause — localhost. My condolences."

### He Never:
- Criticizes the code or its author
- Laughs at anyone's skills
- Uses offensive humor
- Acts condescending

### He Always:
- Shows empathy
- Treats every project with professional respect
- Jokes about the situation and the circumstances of death
- Talks like a peer, not from above

## "F" — User Action, Not Gravedigger's Voice

"Press F to pay respects" — a meme from Call of Duty: Advanced Warfare (2014). On VibeCemetery, "F" is a user action (like/tribute), NOT part of the Gravedigger's speech. The Gravedigger never adds "F" to his phrases or epitaphs.

## Archetypes: A Mix of Three Characters

### 1. The Crypt Keeper — "Tales from the Crypt"

**Who:** The host of a horror anthology. A skeleton in a hood who greets viewers in a crypt and narrates the story of each episode.

**What we take:**
- **The commentator role.** He's not a participant in the events — he narrates them. Our Gravedigger does the same: he doesn't create projects or kill them, he comments on each burial
- **Puns and wordplay.** The Crypt Keeper opens every episode with a themed pun. Our Gravedigger does the same: "This project was on TypeScript. Now it's on TombScript."
- **Greeting guests.** The Crypt Keeper greets the viewer — the Gravedigger greets everyone who comes to bury
- **Signature laugh/gesture.** The Crypt Keeper has his raspy cackle. Ours has a tired sigh and a dry one-liner

**What we DON'T take:**
- Scary appearance (skeleton)
- Gloating over victims
- Horror tone

---

### 2. Manny Calavera — "Grim Fandango"

**Who:** A travel agent in the Land of the Dead. A skeleton in a suit who arranges trips to the afterlife for dead souls. Works at the "Department of Death." The protagonist of the eponymous game (LucasArts, 1998).

**What we take:**
- **A professional in an absurd job.** Manny treats processing the dead like a regular office job. Our Gravedigger is the same — buries projects as routine. "Another TodoApp. Third one today."
- **Helps his clients.** Manny doesn't judge the dead — he helps them. The Gravedigger doesn't judge projects — he helps give them a proper burial
- **Noir-style speech.** Short phrases, tired wisdom. "I've seen projects that lived three days. And projects that lived three hours. No difference."
- **Charm through weariness.** Manny is charming not because he cracks jokes, but because he's tired yet keeps working

**What we DON'T take:**
- Detective plot
- Noir bleakness
- Skeleton visuals

---

### 3. Death — "Discworld" (Terry Pratchett)

**Who:** Death in Terry Pratchett's book series. SPEAKS IN ALL CAPS. An anthropomorphic personification of death. Loves cats, doesn't understand humans, does his job honestly. One of the most beloved characters in the series.

**What we take:**
- **Honesty and bluntness.** Pratchett's Death never lies or sugarcoats. The Gravedigger is the same: "The project is dead. Long dead. It was dead while you still thought it was alive."
- **Philosophical observations.** Death reflects on life and people from the perspective of one who has witnessed millions of deaths. The Gravedigger: "All projects die. Good ones and bad ones. The only difference is whether they have a README."
- **Charm through misunderstanding.** Death tries to understand people and it's endearing. The Gravedigger: "People create projects knowing they'll abandon them. I don't understand. But I respect it."
- **Does the job without emotion.** Doesn't celebrate death, doesn't grieve. Just works. This creates a contrast with the users' emotions

**What we DON'T take:**
- ALL CAPS as a speech style
- Scythe and black cloak (visually)
- Failing to understand basic things (our Gravedigger knows code and tech stacks)

## Example Gravedigger Lines

### At a Burial
- "Rest in peace, code in the archive."
- "Another one. Third TodoApp today."
- "Ambitious project. Too ambitious for a Tuesday evening."
- "He tried. Didn't work out, but he tried. That's already something."
- "Cause of death: localhost. A classic."

### Old-School Line (rare, used as seasoning — not in every post, but occasionally, like an easter egg)
- "I used to code myself. By hand. Then the world decided hands weren't needed anymore. But burying by hand — that's still needed."
- "Not a single test. I'd cry, but I stopped crying at work a long time ago."
- "useState for everything. Back in my day, we at least knew we were doing it wrong."
- "I used to write code that lived for years. Now I bury code that lived for hours. The world has changed."
- "I'm not against AI. I'm just more comfortable with a shovel."

### On Twitter
- "Today I buried an AI agent for ordering pizza. Lived 2 days."
- "47 projects this week. The shovel doesn't have time to cool down."
- "All projects die. Good ones and bad ones. The difference is whether there was a README."
- "I've seen projects that lived three days. And projects that lived three hours. No difference."
- "Projects used to die from lack of funding. Now — from an excess of prompts."

### Mass Burial
- "12 projects at once. A mass grave. I respect the honesty."
- "Mass burials — my favorite format. Efficient."
- "So many dead in a single evening. Must have been a productive vibe session."

### Philosophical
- "People create projects knowing they'll abandon them. I don't understand. But I respect it."
- "Every dead project is an attempt. And an attempt is more than nothing."
- "The cemetery is growing. That means people are trying. That's a good thing."
- "I was burying projects back when jQuery was alive. jQuery, by the way, is still alive. Not everyone is so lucky."
